Nature · Ocean

The Ocean's Hidden World

The deep ocean is one of Earth's least explored environments.

Sunlight fades rapidly as you descend into the ocean. Deep-sea animals have adapted to darkness, cold temperatures and enormous pressure.

How do they find food?

Some eat marine snow, the organic material drifting down from above. Others hunt or live near hydrothermal vents. Researchers use underwater robots and submersibles to study these environments.

Space · Planets

The Planet Where a Day Lasts Longer Than a Year

Venus rotates so slowly that one rotation takes longer than its year.

Venus takes about 243 Earth days to rotate once relative to distant stars, while its orbit around the Sun takes about 225 Earth days.

Why is Venus so hot?

Its thick carbon-dioxide atmosphere traps heat through an intense greenhouse effect. Venus also rotates in the opposite direction to most planets.

Nature · Animals

The Animal With Three Hearts

Octopuses have three hearts and an unusual nervous system.

Two hearts pump blood through the gills, where oxygen enters the blood. The third pumps blood to the rest of the body.

What makes octopuses special?

They can solve certain puzzles, open containers and change their skin colour and texture to blend into their surroundings.

History · Ancient world

The Mystery of the Egyptian Pyramids

Discover what archaeology reveals about these ancient structures.

The Great Pyramid of Giza was built for Pharaoh Khufu more than 4,500 years ago. It originally stood about 146.6 metres tall.

How were they built?

Evidence points to organised teams, quarries, transport routes and carefully planned construction. Researchers continue to investigate the exact techniques used.

Science · Weather

The Science Behind Lightning

A flash of lightning is a powerful electrical discharge during a storm.

Collisions between ice particles and water droplets in storm clouds help separate electrical charges. A discharge can occur when the electric field becomes strong enough.

Why do we hear thunder?

Lightning heats the surrounding air very quickly. The air expands rapidly, creating the sound wave we hear as thunder. Always seek shelter indoors during a thunderstorm.

Space · Saturn

The Planet With Spectacular Rings

Saturn's rings are made of countless separate particles.

Saturn's rings contain pieces of water ice, rocky material and dust. Their particles range from tiny grains to larger chunks.

Are the rings solid?

No. They look like solid bands from far away, but consist of separate particles orbiting Saturn. Scientists study them to learn about gravity and planetary history.

Nature · Insects

How Bees Find Their Way Home

Honeybees use the Sun and landmarks to help them navigate.

Bees use the Sun's position, visual landmarks and an internal sense of direction to navigate between flowers and their hive.

How do bees share information?

A honeybee can perform a waggle dance to communicate the direction and approximate distance of a food source to other bees.

Science · Atmosphere

The Northern Lights Explained

Charged particles interacting with Earth's atmosphere create colourful auroras.

The aurora borealis, or northern lights, occurs when energetic charged particles interact with gases high in Earth's atmosphere. The southern counterpart is the aurora australis.

Where do the colours come from?

Different gases and altitudes produce different colours. Oxygen often produces green or red light, while nitrogen can contribute blue and purple.

Science · Microscopic life

The Tiny Animal That Can Survive Extreme Conditions

Tardigrades are microscopic animals known for surviving certain harsh conditions.

Tardigrades, also called water bears, live in environments ranging from moss to marine habitats.

How can they survive?

Some species enter a dormant state called cryptobiosis when conditions become harsh. They can endure certain periods of drying and other environmental stresses, but they are not indestructible.

Space · The universe

How Big Is the Universe?

The observable universe contains an extraordinary number of galaxies and stars.

The universe is about 13.8 billion years old. The observable universe is estimated to be about 93 billion light-years across.

Why is it wider than 27.6 billion light-years?

Space has expanded while light was travelling towards us. The entire universe may extend far beyond what we can observe, and scientists do not yet know whether it is finite or infinite.
